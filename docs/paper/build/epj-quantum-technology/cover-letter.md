Dear Editors,

We are submitting *"Three Axes of Quantum Risk: A Unified
Observability Model for PQC, QKD, and Crypto-Agility"* for
consideration in *EPJ Quantum Technology* as a Research
article.

PQ-readiness scanners today answer one question: is the
primitive quantum-resistant? Two assets that look identical
under that question can have very different migration costs,
and very different channel-level protections, neither of
which the scanner sees. The paper treats quantum-risk
posture as a three-axis problem instead, scoring each asset
on algorithmic resistance, channel protection, and migration
agility, then folding the three into a deadline-adjusted
risk score $q$. We validate all three axes with reproducible
studies — a Tranco-top-1k TLS survey (43.8% hybrid PQ key
exchange), a controlled ETSI GS QKD 014 emulator study (13 of
13 induced link/KME transitions classified correctly), and an
11-project crypto-agility pilot (91% agreement with
hand-graded ground truth) — and release the reference
implementation end to end: eBPF-based TLS observation, a
working ETSI GS QKD 014 collector with a Key-Management-Entity
emulator, and a static crypto-agility scanner. Every artifact
is open at <https://github.com/e2esolutions-tech/ree0xQ>
under MIT.

Why EPJ Quantum Technology. The deployment side of quantum
technology is where this work lives: the QKD axis treats
channel-level quantum-secure key delivery as a measured
property of an asset rather than a separate policy track, and
the KME emulator lets practitioners reproduce the
channel-state classification results on one Linux host with
no QKD hardware. The journal's coverage of deployed QKD
networks and their operational telemetry — work we build on
directly in the manuscript — makes it the natural home for a
measurable, QKD-aware posture model paired with a
reproducible test rig.
